An open bench

Questions I wanted answered, so I measured them.

This is a workshop, not a portfolio. I take a question about how deep learning systems actually behave — serving, deployment, the cost of going fast — and I run the experiment on hardware sitting in my room until there is a number. Measured, not asserted.

A lot of what ends up here is stuff the literature skips. Everyone benchmarks the model; almost nobody publishes what it costs to deploy one. The work in progress is posted alongside the finished work, including the experiments that failed.

On the bench

open questions

The queue, in the open. These are unanswered — no numbers are claimed for anything on this list. Some are next up, some are blocked on a harness that cannot yet measure them honestly.

  • Next up

    Do application-level tricks dwarf everything in the serving layer?

    Detect every N frames and track between them, tile the ROI for small objects, cascade a cheap detector into an expensive classifier. These routinely beat every serving optimization I have measured by an order of magnitude — and not one of them is measured yet. This is the most important gap in the CV study.

  • In design

    How far can vision tokens be cut before the answer falls off a cliff?

    Prefill cost scales with token count, and token count is a free variable. The hypothesis is that natural-image VQA tolerates aggressive pruning and document VQA does not — so the honest output is a per-task frontier rather than one number.

  • Queued

    What does a VLA policy cost per control cycle?

    A robot's loop has a deadline that does not negotiate. Serving metrics built for throughput do not describe this at all: what matters is the tail, and what the tail costs when the policy has to fit on hardware the robot carries.

  • Queued

    How do you find a bottleneck you did not already suspect?

    Everything published so far measures known stages. That teaches you to confirm a hypothesis, not to discover one. Nsight Systems with NVTX ranges is the obvious answer and it deserves its own write-up.

  • Blocked

    Is DeepStream genuinely source-bound, or is my harness the bottleneck?

    At 8 streams it reaches ~501 fps against 1640 — which looks decisive until the GPU shows median 0% utilisation. A 345 KB CPU copy per frame per stream is in the critical path. Fixing it means pre-allocated NVMM buffers, not a parameter change. Until then the number means nothing and is not published as one.

  • Blocked

    Can the dynamic-batch server flow use CUDA graphs?

    Graphs gave +11.8% fps elsewhere. The batching flow needs one captured graph per batch size via graph_spec, which is why the tuning sweep could not cover it either.

Things nobody benchmarks

Every one of these decides whether a deployment works, and none of them appear in the benchmark tables people actually publish. They get their own write-ups here.

01

Cold start and engine load

Everyone reports the thousandth frame. Nobody reports how long you wait for the first one — or what that means for a service that scales to zero.

02

Engine portability

A TensorRT engine is welded to the exact build that produced it. Two containers in one study already disagreed — 10.7 against 10.3 — and that is an ops problem, not a footnote.

03

Memory per instance

Doubling server instances bought +30% throughput. The VRAM it cost is the number that decides whether the config fits on the card at all.

04

Clocks, power and heat

A benchmark's first thirty seconds are not its steady state. Sustained throughput on a throttling card is a different number, and it is the one you deploy against.

05

Whether the benchmark is real

A harness that cannot saturate the system measures the harness. This one got that wrong once and had to throw the first answer away — which is why saturation is now a precondition, not a detail.

06

The experiments that failed

2:4 structured sparsity returned +1.1% at its ceiling — not worth the retraining. Published anyway. A cheap negative result closes a question for everyone who would have tried it next.

The bar for a number here

One variable at a time

Every arm shares the same model file, the same hardware and the same harness, so a difference in the table is a difference in the thing under test.

Saturate before you measure

A benchmark that does not saturate the source measures the source. Getting this wrong once is why the first answer in the CV study had to be thrown away.

Publish the cost, not just the win

Speed is bought with accuracy, memory or complexity. Every result states what it cost, with the accuracy delta beside the throughput number.

Say what was not measured

Open questions stay marked open and unmeasured claims are not made. Where the rig has a hard limit, it is stated rather than quietly skipped.
