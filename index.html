An open bench · est. 2026

Questions I wanted answered, so I measured them.

Sadbodh Research Notebook · github.com/sadbodhs

This is a workshop, not a portfolio. I take a question about how deep learning systems actually behave — serving, deployment, the cost of going fast — and run the experiment on hardware sitting in my room until there is a number. Measured, not asserted. A lot of what lands here is what the literature skips: everyone benchmarks the model, almost nobody publishes what it costs to deploy one. Work in progress is posted alongside finished work, including the experiments that failed.

On the bench

04 open · 01 half measured · 01 answered

The queue, in the open. Most of these are unanswered and claim no numbers. Some are next up; some are blocked on a harness that cannot yet measure them honestly. Where one has been partly measured, the measured half says so and the open half stays open — a question is not closed because the easy side of it fell over.

Half measured

Do application-level tricks dwarf everything in the serving layer?

Detect every N frames and track between them, tile the ROI for small objects, cascade a cheap detector into an expensive classifier. These routinely beat every serving optimization I have measured by an order of magnitude. Until the cascade below, not one of them had been measured. This is the largest gap in the CV study.

Half measured: yes — but by 1.6–2×, not an order of magnitude. On MEVA surveillance video with YOLO and a 7B VLM running live on the same 3090, a detector gate takes the card from 5 to 8 cameras, and to 10 with ROI crops and a TensorRT detector — keeping 97–99% of annotated activities in view for about one point of recognition. The savings belong to the scene: 98% of VLM calls on an empty camera, 12–15% on a busy one. Three surprises: a Poisson capacity model undercounted the camera fleet by more than 2×; the detector competes with the VLM for the GPU, so TensorRT buys two cameras; and the weak link is the VLM, which sees doorways and car doors but not phones or conversations at that distance. Rebuilt the industry way — DeepStream, a batched detector and a tracker — the CV side stops competing for the GPU, which buys one more camera and leaves the VLM as the only limit. Still open: detect-then-track and ROI tiling on the CV side. The live cascade →

Answered

How far can vision tokens be cut before the answer falls off a cliff?

Prefill cost scales with token count, and token count is a free variable. The hypothesis was that natural-image VQA tolerates aggressive pruning and document VQA does not — so the honest output is a per-task frontier rather than one number.

It holds, for a different reason than predicted. At ~100 vision tokens, VQAv2 (no text at all) keeps 91% of its peak accuracy [87, 95]; scene text (TextVQA) keeps 60%; charts and documents keep about a third. DocVQA needs ~1,000 tokens to saturate, ChartQA 577. The first attempt used TextVQA as the "natural image" arm and got the order wrong — it is still a reading task. What governs the budget is not the image category but the size of the smallest feature that carries the answer. Not yet tested: high-resolution appearance tasks (small, distant objects), which that rule predicts behave like reading. The per-task frontier →

Queued

What does a VLA policy cost per control cycle?

A robot's loop has a deadline that does not negotiate. Serving metrics built for throughput do not describe this at all: what matters is the tail, and what the tail costs when the policy has to fit on hardware the robot carries.

Queued

How do you find a bottleneck you did not already suspect?

Everything published so far measures known stages. That teaches you to confirm a hypothesis, not to discover one. Nsight Systems with NVTX ranges is the obvious answer and it deserves its own write-up.

Blocked

Is DeepStream genuinely source-bound, or is my harness the bottleneck?

At 8 streams it reaches ~501 fps against 1640 — which looks decisive until the GPU shows median 0% utilisation. A 345 KB CPU copy per frame per stream sits in the critical path. Fixing it means pre-allocated NVMM buffers, not a parameter change. Until then the number means nothing and is not published as one.

Blocked

Can the dynamic-batch server flow use CUDA graphs?

Graphs gave +11.8% fps elsewhere. The batching flow needs one captured graph per batch size via graph_spec, which is why the tuning sweep could not cover it either.

Things nobody benchmarks

Every one of these decides whether a deployment works, and none of them appear in the benchmark tables people actually publish. They get their own write-ups here.

01

Cold start and engine load

Everyone reports the thousandth frame. Nobody reports how long you wait for the first one — or what that means for a service that scales to zero.

02

Engine portability

A TensorRT engine is welded to the exact build that produced it. Two containers in one study already disagreed — 10.7 against 10.3 — and that is an ops problem, not a footnote.

03

Memory per instance

Doubling server instances bought +30% throughput. The VRAM it cost is the number that decides whether the config fits on the card at all.

04

Clocks, power and heat

A benchmark's first thirty seconds are not its steady state. Sustained throughput on a throttling card is a different number, and it is the one you deploy against.

05

Whether the benchmark is real

A harness that cannot saturate the system measures the harness. This one got that wrong once and had to throw the first answer away.

06

The experiments that failed

2:4 structured sparsity returned +1.1% at its ceiling — not worth the retraining. Published anyway. A cheap negative result closes a question for everyone.

4

The bar for a number here

R1

One variable at a time

Every arm shares the same model file, the same hardware and the same harness, so a difference in the table is a difference in the thing under test.

R2

Saturate before you measure

A benchmark that does not saturate the source measures the source. Getting this wrong once is why the first answer in the CV study had to be thrown away.

R3

Publish the cost, not just the win

Speed is bought with accuracy, memory or complexity. Every result states what it cost, with the accuracy delta beside the throughput number.

R4

Say what was not measured

Open questions stay marked open and unmeasured claims are not made. Where the rig has a hard limit, it is stated rather than quietly skipped.
